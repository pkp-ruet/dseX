Portfolio, made simple

Most readers come to the portfolio page with three questions. Today the page answers them, but the answers are spread over many cards, folds and words. This plan puts the three answers at the top, shows each share once, and moves everything else one tap away.

What makes it hard today

I read the page as it is built now (the share list, the verdict, plan and area cards, the money tabs). Five things make it hard to take in:

  • 1
    Five ways to judge the same thing.A grade letter (C), an answer word ("Needs work"), four area lights, a group word on each share and an action word on each share. A new reader can't tell which one matters.
  • 2
    One share shows up in up to five places.The share list, "Your way back", "Three changes to an A", "Each of your shares" and the questions behind the areas. The reader has to put the story of one share together.
  • 3
    The plan is three cards, each with its own idea.Way back, path to an A and each share all say "what to do", in three different shapes and with new words (Switch, Trim, path, way back).
  • 4
    Too many controls.Six chips at the top, seven buttons in an open share row, six money tabs, a sort menu, a search and filter chips.
  • 5
    The free card is a page of its own.Four numbered parts, examples, a "you have now" list and a sticky price column. It explains Premium well, but it is the longest thing on the page.

The idea: answer three questions, in order

A general reader, whether they bought their first share last month or hold twenty, wants the same three answers. The page should give them top to bottom, each in one card, before anything else.

1How much is my money worth?Value, today's move and profit since you bought. Free.
2Is everything okay?One grade and four plain checks with a tick or a warning. Grade free, checks Premium.
3What should I do?At most three things, most important first, each with the taka involved. Premium.

Then Your shares (each share once, with one status), then More details folded. Everything else on the page today moves into a share's sheet or behind a "Why?" tap.

The rules this plan follows

These are the guideline for the page, and for any part added to it later.

  • 1
    One verdict.The grade letter plus one word ("Grade C · Needs some work"). The separate answer card and the four area tiles become four checklist rows under the grade, each one sentence with a tick or a warning.
  • 2
    Each share once, with one status.Three statuses only: All good (green), Watch (amber, with the reason: results Thu, near a warning line) and Act (red, with the verb: sell some, swap, read the warning, buy more). A free reader sees our group word in the same place.
  • 3
    At most three things to do.One list, most taka first. Each item is one sentence, the money involved and one link. It replaces "Your way back", "Three changes to an A" and "Each of your shares". The grade change sits under the list as one line.
  • 4
    A share's whole story lives in its sheet.Our take, the price that gets your money back, the facts and the buttons. Bought more and Sold are the two buttons; the rest go under "More".
  • 5
    Explanations sit behind a tap.Each checklist row opens a sheet with the questions behind it (today's area folds). Nothing that explains "why" is open on the page.
  • 6
    Details are one fold with four tabs.Growth (value over time, vs the market) · Where it sits (by share and by business) · Income (money made, dividends) · Activity (history, news, statement).
  • 7
    One look.One card style, the same row shape everywhere (name left, taka right), colour only for good, watch and bad, clay only on things you tap, gold only for Premium. No sticky chip row: the page is short enough to scroll.

Helping a first-time reader

  • 1
    Empty page = three steps."Add your shares → see your grade and what to do → we tell you when something changes", with one Add button and a link to a sample.
  • 2
    One small note the first time the check appears."Your page has three parts: your money, your grade, what to do. Tap any share for its full story." Shown once, closed with ✕, remembered on the device.
  • 3
    Every number says what it is.No bare figures: "৳1,050 cash a year", not "৳1,050". Any market word gets a tap explanation, like on the stock page.

How to build it

Five steps. The analysis itself (lib/portfolio-report.ts, the grade, the backend) stays as it is; this is about what the page shows and where.

  1. 1 · Money and "Is everything okay?"Hero down to value, today and since you bought. Verdict + areas become one card: grade line + four checklist rows; each row opens a sheet with that area's questions.
    PortfolioHero, PfVerdict → new PfHealth, PfArea becomes the sheet's body
  2. 2 · "What to do"One list of at most three, built from the path steps, the share actions and the way-back problem shares, one decision per share (the rule already holds). The C → B line under it. The what-if card moves into Details.
    new PfTodo + a todo() builder in lib/portfolio-report.ts; PfPlan retired
  3. 3 · Shares with one status, and the share sheetRows lose the inline drawer; a tap opens a ui/Sheet with our take, getting back what you paid, facts, Bought more / Sold and "More". Search and filters stay for 8+ shares.
    PortfolioClient rows, new PfShareSheet
  4. 4 · More detailsSix tabs become four; the chip row goes; corrections move into Activity.
    PfMoneyRoom, DashSectionNav removed from this page
  5. 5 · Free reader, first visit, samplesLocked checklist rows, the "What to do" box as the one Premium card, the first-visit note, the empty state, and the sample pages on the same layout.
    PortfolioPromo shortened, sample portfolio pages

Each step ships on its own and leaves the page working. Before and after, compare: time on the page, taps on "What to do" items, share sheet opens, and premium_lock_tap from the portfolio.

Decisions for you

1. The free reader's Premium cardRecommended: short

Short: the "What to do" box shown above (count, grade change, three ticks, one button), with the four-part explanation moved to /premium. Or keep the long card from 12 Nov and only move it lower. The short card fits this plan; the long one explains more.

2. Share status wordsRecommended: All good / Watch / Act

Or keep today's action words alone (Sell, Buy more, Switch) and show nothing for the rest. Three statuses give every share an answer.

3. The sticky chip rowRecommended: remove it

With three cards on top and details folded, the page is short. Or keep three chips: Money · Shares · Details.

4. Order of shares and the checkRecommended: check first

The plan puts "Is everything okay?" and "What to do" above the shares (on 4 Nov you chose shares right under the money). Check first answers the reader's questions in order; shares first keeps the list where people are used to it.
